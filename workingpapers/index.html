  1. Working Paper
    How EU place-based policies shape discourse on the EU: Analysis of communication by political candidates (R&R)
    Johannes Lattmann
    Abs

    This article explores the relationship between the reception of EU (European Union) place-based policies and political candidates’ political communication about the EU. While it is often assumed that recipients of EU funding adopt more favor- able views, I argue that such support potentially reinforces euroskeptic narratives. In particular, euroskeptic parties leverage the visibility and politicization of EU funding to amplify their opposition to the EU. Drawing on a comprehensive social media dataset from German election candidates’ posts between 2014 and 2021 (N = 4.482.601), this study examines how regional EU funding is associated with candi- dates’ stances and the prominence of EU-related content in their communications. To assess levels of euroskepticism, I develop a transformer-based supervised clas- sification model capable of detecting a variety of political stances toward the EU. I find that EU funding is associated with higher levels of EU politicization among political candidates, but only in eastern Germany. Also, across all specifications my results suggest that EU funding is positively associated with more eurosceptic posts for candidates from the Alternative for Germany (AfD), drawing a pessimistic imagine of EU funding as a possible solution to moderate eurosceptic parties.

  2. Working Paper
    A False Promise for the Left Behind: The Uneven Political Impact of EU Place-Based Policies (In Review, preprint available)
    Johannes Lattmann and Valentin Lang
    Abs PDF

    To counter regionally concentrated political frustration, place-based economic policies targeting disadvantaged regions have become increasingly popular among policymakers. This paper examines the effectiveness of place-based policies in mitigating political frustration and populism with a focus on their distributional effects within regions. We argue that place-based policies are unlikely to benefit the most disadvantaged voters due to a skill bias. Empirically, we study the world’s largest place-based policy in the context of the European Union (EU) using a natural experiment and randomized survey experiments. We construct a 1980-2020 dataset with subnational geocodes at the individual level (N=1.4 million) across the EU and leverage a discontinuity in regional funding eligibility in an RD design. In experiments embedded in a two-wave survey (N=2,046), we expose respondents to information and encouragement treatments related to the policy. Both analyses show that place-based policies reduce political discontent only among the highly skilled and the rich, while political attitudes of other groups are not affected. These results highlight that the effectiveness of place-based policies in addressing political discontent is severely limited by their distributional effects.

  3. Working Paper
    Misperception about Campaign Cost and Candidate Entry: Evidence from French Elections
    Johannes Stupperich and Johannes Lattmann
    Abs

    Do candidates misperceive campaign cost when they decide to stand for elec- tion? To answer this question we differentiate conceptually between ex-ante and ex-post cost and apply this framework to French legislative elections. Ex-ante costs need to be paid before candidates stand, whereas ex-post cost are due af- ter the campaign and thus can be misperceived at the time of running. One such ex-post cost — the requirement to file a campaign account which results in inel- igibility in case of non-compliance — only affects French candidates who gained more than 1% of the vote share. We exploit this discontinuity and estimate that at the threshold at least 7.4% of candidates under-estimate this type of campaign cost related to administrative hurdles. In contrast, the sanctioning probability falls by 2.8 percentage points at the more salient 5% threshold advertised clearly by the government. Consistent with our misperception theory, sanctioned candidates are on average less experienced and less likely to be supported by a party than their non-sanctioned counterparts. Before a 2011 reform, which extended ineligibility to any kind of elections, sanctioned candidates were more likely to subsequently con- test local elections. While previous literature suggests that politicians misperceive constituency preferences, we show that beyond that, some candidates misperceive the “rules” of electoral competition. We conclude with implications for the number of candidates and a discussion of the relevance of small candidates for electoral competition.

  4. Working Paper
    Heterogeneity in the Political and Economic Effect of EU Place-Based Policy Schemes: A Supply-Side Analysis in German Municipalities
    Johannes Lattmann
    Abs

    Place-based policies are often depicted as a means of decreasing resentment by improving economic conditions in left-behind places. Yet, little attention had been paid to the heterogeneity of funds and their respective ends. Relying on 335,000 projects by the European Cohesion Policy for the funding period 2014-2020 in Germany, I investigate how different funding types impact the vote share for the eurosceptic party AfD and support for the EU. First, for the electoral analysis, I use a fine-grained municipality level-dataset (n = 48,045). Second, to further investigate the mechanism of my argument, I harmonized two GLES election datasets (n = 12,000) for examining individual-level data. I find that public good projects and direct firm support are especially potent in improving firm tax revenues. Nevertheless, it is projects dedicated to individual support or public goods that significantly decrease AfD vote share. Lastly, I show that the effect on project type on EU support is depending on the income-level of citizens.

  5. Detecting EU sentiment in texts: A LLM Machine Learning application for Euroscepticism research
    Johannes Lattmann
    preprint,
    Abs PDF

    This article presents a supervised classification model for measuring mentions and sentiments about the European Union. This research provides new ways to study and explain variation in Euroscepticism. Specifically, this research presents a binary classification algorithm for detecting speech about the European Union. In addition, it introduces a second classifier consisting of a multi-class classification model for EU praise, hard eurosceptic sentiment, policy scepticism and demand for further EU integration. These are widely used concepts in Euroscepticism research. Based on a pre-trained BERT-based model, I fine tune my classification models on 2000 human annotated tweets of German election candidates between 2007-2021. I am further refining this by training additional models on parliamentary speeches, in order to expand the potential texts to be analysed with these models. All models are available in German and English. This new classification application can expand the possibilities for researchers to study how politicians write and speak about the EU.
